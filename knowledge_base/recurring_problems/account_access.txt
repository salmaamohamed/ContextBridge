TITLE: Account Access Recurring Problems
DOCUMENT TYPE: Company Recurring Problem Knowledge
SCOPE: All Departments
STATUS: Approved
OWNER: IT Operations
VERSION: 1.0

1. PURPOSE

This document describes recurring account-related problems that employees may experience during onboarding or normal work. It provides general troubleshooting guidance for company accounts without replacing the official Access Request Process or Security policies.

The purpose is to help employees identify common account problems, perform safe checks, and determine when an issue should be escalated.

This document does not contain passwords, credentials, tokens, or project-specific access information.

2. PROBLEM: ACCOUNT HAS NOT BEEN CREATED

Symptoms:
- Employee cannot sign in because no company account exists.
- The employee has not received an account activation message.
- The employee's manager expected access to be available but no account is visible.

Possible causes:
- Account creation has not been completed.
- Onboarding information is incomplete.
- The request is still pending.
- The account invitation was sent to an incorrect address.

Troubleshooting:
1. Confirm that the employee's onboarding information was submitted.
2. Confirm the expected company email or username.
3. Check whether an account-creation request exists.
4. Check the approved access-request status.
5. If the request is pending, follow the documented access process.
6. If the request was completed but no account exists, contact the responsible IT owner.

Expected result:
The employee receives an individual company account and can complete the approved activation process.

Escalation:
Escalate when account creation was approved or expected but has not been completed.

3. PROBLEM: WRONG COMPANY ACCOUNT

Symptoms:
- The employee is logged into a personal account.
- The employee can access public resources but not company resources.
- The system displays a different organization or account.

Possible causes:
- Browser session is using a personal account.
- Multiple accounts are signed in.
- The employee used a personal email instead of the company account.

Troubleshooting:
1. Check the account currently signed into the service.
2. Sign out of the incorrect account when appropriate.
3. Sign in using the approved company account.
4. Retry the requested operation.
5. If access is still unavailable, check the access-request status.

Do not share company credentials with another person.

4. PROBLEM: PASSWORD OR LOGIN FAILURE

Symptoms:
- Username and password are rejected.
- Login repeatedly fails.
- The employee cannot remember the account password.

Troubleshooting:
1. Confirm that the correct company account is being used.
2. Check for obvious username or typing errors.
3. Use the company's approved password-reset mechanism.
4. Complete any required identity verification.
5. Retry the login using the newly established credentials.
6. If the account remains unavailable, contact the approved support channel.

Employees must not ask another employee for their password or attempt to bypass authentication.

5. PROBLEM: MULTI-FACTOR AUTHENTICATION FAILURE

Symptoms:
- MFA code is rejected.
- Authentication approval is not received.
- The registered authentication method is unavailable.
- The employee cannot complete the second authentication step.

Safe checks:
1. Confirm the employee is using the registered authentication method.
2. Check whether the device has connectivity when required.
3. Check the device time if the authentication method depends on synchronized time.
4. Retry the approved authentication flow.
5. Use the documented recovery process if the registered method is unavailable.

Do not disable MFA to solve an onboarding problem unless an authorized administrator follows the approved procedure.

6. PROBLEM: ACCOUNT LOCKED

Symptoms:
- The system reports that the account is locked.
- Login attempts are blocked after repeated failures.

Possible causes:
- Repeated incorrect credentials.
- Automated security protection.
- Suspicious authentication activity.

Troubleshooting:
1. Stop repeated login attempts.
2. Follow the approved account-unlock or password-reset process.
3. If suspicious activity is suspected, report it through the security process.
4. Contact IT support if the account remains locked.

Do not attempt to bypass an account lock.

7. PROBLEM: INVITATION NOT RECEIVED

Symptoms:
- An expected account invitation is not present.
- The employee cannot activate the account.

Troubleshooting:
1. Confirm the expected company email address.
2. Check the appropriate inbox and approved filtered-mail locations.
3. Confirm that the invitation was actually generated.
4. Request a resend through the approved process if available.
5. Escalate if the invitation cannot be generated or repeatedly fails to arrive.

Do not use an unofficial invitation or forward an activation link to another person.

8. PROBLEM: ACCESS WAS APPROVED BUT LOGIN STILL FAILS

Symptoms:
- The employee has an approved access request.
- The service still rejects the login or reports insufficient permission.

Possible causes:
- Provisioning has not completed.
- The wrong account is being used.
- The permission was granted to a different account.
- The system requires an additional activation step.

Troubleshooting:
1. Confirm the approved account.
2. Confirm the requested system.
3. Check the request status.
4. Complete any activation instructions.
5. Retry using the company account.
6. Contact the system owner if the approved permission is not available.

9. PROBLEM: ACCOUNT EXISTS BUT PROFILE INFORMATION IS INCORRECT

Symptoms:
- Name, department or other profile information is incorrect.
- The employee appears under the wrong organizational information.

Troubleshooting:
1. Confirm the incorrect information.
2. Check whether the service allows approved profile updates.
3. If the profile is controlled centrally, contact the responsible owner.
4. Do not create a duplicate account simply to correct profile information.

10. PROBLEM: ACCOUNT WORKS ON ONE DEVICE BUT NOT ANOTHER

Possible causes:
- Different account is active on the second device.
- Browser session is outdated.
- Device configuration differs.
- Authentication method is unavailable on the second device.

Troubleshooting:
1. Confirm the same company account is being used.
2. Sign out of an incorrect account.
3. Retry the approved login flow.
4. Check the required authentication method.
5. If the issue continues on only one device, provide device and application details when escalating.

11. INFORMATION TO PROVIDE WHEN ESCALATING

Provide:
- Employee name or approved employee identifier.
- Department and team.
- System name.
- Company account or username, but never the password.
- Error message.
- Time the problem occurred.
- Steps already attempted.
- Access-request reference when available.
- Device or browser information when relevant.

Never include:
- Passwords.
- MFA codes.
- API keys.
- Access tokens.
- Private keys.

12. WHEN TO ESCALATE

Escalate when:
- Account creation is incomplete after approval.
- Password recovery does not work.
- MFA recovery is unavailable.
- The account remains locked.
- Approved access is not reflected.
- The issue may involve unauthorized access.
- The employee cannot safely determine the next step.

Security concerns should follow the company's Incident Reporting Process.

13. CONTEXTBRIDGE AGENT USAGE

The Onboarding Support Agent can use this document for questions such as:
- "My company account has not been created. What should I do?"
- "I cannot log in."
- "My MFA is not working."
- "I received an access approval but still cannot sign in."
- "What information should I provide to IT?"

The agent should provide documented troubleshooting steps and escalate when the problem exceeds the employee's authorized actions.

The agent must never request or expose passwords, MFA codes, tokens or private keys.

14. RELATION TO COMPANY KNOWLEDGE

This document describes recurring account problems.

The general approval and access workflow is defined by the company's Access Request Process.

Security-related concerns should also use the Incident Reporting Process when applicable.

Project-specific permissions are outside the scope of this document.
