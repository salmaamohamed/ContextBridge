TITLE: Common Onboarding and Technical FAQs
DOCUMENT TYPE: Company FAQ Knowledge
SCOPE: All Departments
STATUS: Approved
OWNER: IT Operations + People Operations
VERSION: 1.0

1. PURPOSE

This document contains frequently asked general questions that new employees may have during onboarding and normal work.

The answers are company-wide guidance. They are intentionally not tied to a particular project, repository, database or application.

2. HOW DO I START MY ONBOARDING?

Start with the onboarding instructions provided by the company and complete the required tasks in the order specified by the onboarding owner.

If a required task is blocked by missing access, follow the Access Request Process while continuing with other approved tasks that do not depend on the unavailable access.

3. WHO SHOULD I CONTACT IF I AM BLOCKED?

Use the responsible support channel defined for the problem.

For account or access problems, use the approved IT or system-owner process.

For security concerns, use the Incident Reporting Process.

For project-specific technical questions, use the project support path defined by the relevant project documentation.

If you are unsure which team owns the issue, provide the system name, problem description and any available reference number.

4. HOW DO I REQUEST SYSTEM ACCESS?

Use the approved company access-request mechanism.

Include:
- Your department and role.
- System or resource needed.
- Required permission.
- Business reason.
- Project or team when relevant.
- Required date.
- Manager or owner information when required.

Do not request access simply because another employee has it. Access should match business need.

5. WHAT IF MY ACCESS REQUEST IS STILL PENDING?

Check the request status first.

If the request is still waiting for approval, contact the appropriate approver according to the documented process.

If approval is complete but access has not been provisioned, contact the responsible system owner or IT support.

Do not submit repeated duplicate requests unless the responsible team instructs you to do so.

6. WHAT SHOULD I DO IF I FORGOT MY PASSWORD?

Use the company's approved password-reset process.

Do not ask a colleague for their password.

Do not create an unofficial workaround.

If password recovery fails, contact the approved support channel and provide the error information without sharing the password.

7. WHAT IF MFA IS NOT WORKING?

Check that you are using the registered authentication method and follow the approved MFA recovery procedure.

Do not disable security controls yourself.

If the registered device or method is unavailable, use the company's documented recovery path or contact IT support.

8. CAN I USE A PERSONAL ACCOUNT FOR COMPANY WORK?

Company work should use the approved company accounts and services.

A personal account should not be used to bypass company access controls or move company information outside approved systems.

If the required company account is unavailable, report the access problem.

9. CAN I SHARE MY ACCOUNT WITH A COLLEAGUE?

No.

Individual company accounts should be used by the assigned employee.

Sharing credentials reduces accountability and can create security risks.

If another employee needs access, they should request their own approved account or permission.

10. WHERE SHOULD I STORE COMPANY CREDENTIALS?

Use the approved company credential-management mechanism.

Do not store passwords, access tokens or private keys in source code, public files, tickets or ordinary chat messages.

11. WHAT SHOULD I DO IF I ACCIDENTALLY SHARE A PASSWORD OR TOKEN?

Treat the credential as potentially exposed.

Do not continue sharing it.

Do not put the exposed secret into a ticket or chat message.

Report the situation through the approved security process and follow the required credential rotation or revocation procedure.

12. WHAT IS AN INCIDENT?

An incident is an unexpected event that may negatively affect company systems, information, employees, customers or operations.

Examples include suspected unauthorized access, exposed credentials, serious service failures and accidental disclosure of sensitive information.

When in doubt about a potential security incident, follow the approved reporting process.

13. WHEN SHOULD I REPORT AN INCIDENT?

Report it as soon as reasonably possible.

You do not need to know the complete root cause before reporting.

Provide the facts you know and avoid unnecessary speculation.

14. WHAT INFORMATION SHOULD I INCLUDE IN AN INCIDENT REPORT?

Include:
- What happened.
- When it happened.
- Affected system.
- Expected behavior.
- Actual behavior.
- Error message.
- Impact.
- Actions already attempted.
- Relevant references.

Do not include passwords, MFA codes, API keys or private keys.

15. WHAT SHOULD I DO IF A COMPANY SYSTEM IS DOWN?

First determine whether the problem is local to your device or appears to affect other users.

Check the approved company communication or status channel when available.

If the outage is significant or you cannot determine the appropriate response, report it through the approved support or incident process.

16. WHAT SHOULD I DO BEFORE MAKING A SIGNIFICANT CHANGE?

Understand the proposed change, expected impact, testing requirements, implementation steps and recovery plan.

Follow the applicable Change Management Process.

Do not make an unapproved change to a controlled environment.

17. WHAT IS A CHANGE REQUEST?

A change request records a proposed modification to a shared system, service, configuration, data structure or business process.

It normally includes the reason, impact, implementation approach, testing, verification and recovery information.

18. DO ALL CHANGES REQUIRE THE SAME APPROVAL?

No.

Approval depends on the type, risk, environment and potential impact of the change.

Standard, normal and emergency changes may follow different approved procedures.

19. WHAT SHOULD I DO IF A CHANGE FAILS?

Stop or limit further changes when appropriate.

Assess the impact and follow the approved recovery or rollback procedure.

Notify the responsible owner.

If the failure causes an incident, also follow the Incident Reporting Process.

20. CAN I FIX A PRODUCTION PROBLEM BY CHANGING CONFIGURATION DIRECTLY?

Do not make an unapproved production change.

Follow the approved incident and change procedures.

Emergency changes may have a separate documented process when immediate action is required.

21. WHAT SHOULD I DO IF I DO NOT UNDERSTAND AN ONBOARDING TASK?

Review the task description and related approved company documentation.

If the task is still unclear, ask the onboarding support channel or responsible owner.

The ContextBridge Onboarding Support Agent can explain documented procedures and point to relevant knowledge.

22. WHAT IF THE DOCUMENTATION SEEMS OUTDATED?

Do not silently replace the documented procedure with an assumption.

Report the outdated or conflicting information to the responsible owner.

Use the currently approved documentation until the owner confirms an update.

23. WHAT IF TWO COMPANY DOCUMENTS CONFLICT?

Check the document owner, version and approval status.

Use the more specific approved procedure when the conflict concerns a specific system or process.

If the conflict cannot be resolved from the documentation, escalate to the responsible owner.

24. CAN I CREATE MY OWN WORKAROUND?

A workaround should not bypass company security, access controls or approved processes.

If a documented workaround exists, follow it.

Otherwise ask the responsible owner before introducing a new process for shared systems.

25. WHAT INFORMATION SHOULD I INCLUDE WHEN ASKING FOR TECHNICAL HELP?

Provide:
- Clear description of the problem.
- Expected result.
- Actual result.
- Error message.
- Relevant environment.
- Steps already attempted.
- Time the issue occurred.
- Related request or ticket reference.

Do not include secrets.

26. HOW SHOULD I COMMUNICATE A TECHNICAL PROBLEM?

Be factual and concise.

A useful message can follow:

Problem:
What is not working?

Expected:
What should happen?

Actual:
What happened instead?

Context:
What system or environment is affected?

Attempts:
What have you already tried?

Impact:
Who or what is blocked?

27. SHOULD I REPORT EVERY ERROR?

Not every minor error is a formal incident.

Use the applicable support, troubleshooting or ticket process for normal technical problems.

Report an incident when the event meets the company's incident criteria or creates a significant security, operational or business concern.

28. CAN THE ONBOARDING AGENT APPROVE ACCESS?

No.

The agent can explain the documented access process and help identify the information required for a request.

Approval remains with the authorized manager, system owner or other responsible approver.

29. CAN THE ONBOARDING AGENT CHANGE MY SYSTEM PERMISSIONS?

No.

The agent can guide the employee through the approved process but should not invent or bypass authorization.

30. WHAT DOES THE ONBOARDING SUPPORT AGENT KNOW?

The agent retrieves approved company and, when appropriate, project knowledge through the RAG system.

Company knowledge includes general policies, processes, recurring problems, FAQs and approved onboarding guidance.

Project-specific answers require project knowledge.

31. WHAT IF THE AGENT CANNOT FIND AN ANSWER?

The agent should say that the available approved knowledge does not provide enough information rather than inventing an answer.

The employee can then escalate to the appropriate human owner.

32. WHAT IF I NEED A PROJECT-SPECIFIC ANSWER?

A project-specific question should be answered using the relevant Project KB.

For example, a general access process belongs to Company KB, while the exact permission required for a particular project repository belongs to Project KB.

33. HOW DOES RAG HELP?

RAG retrieves relevant approved knowledge before the agent generates an answer.

This allows the agent to ground responses in company documentation instead of relying only on general model knowledge.

34. WHAT SHOULD I DO IF I FIND A SECURITY ISSUE IN DOCUMENTATION?

Do not publish sensitive details or credentials.

Report the issue through the approved security process and identify the affected document without unnecessarily copying sensitive information.

35. DOCUMENT GOVERNANCE

These FAQs are approved Company KB content.

FAQs should be updated when company processes change.

If an answer becomes outdated, the responsible owner should review and update the knowledge before it is treated as current guidance.
